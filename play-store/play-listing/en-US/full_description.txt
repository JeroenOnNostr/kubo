Kubo is a video app built around trust, not algorithms. As a parent, you decide exactly which people, feeds, and creators your child can see.

There is no recommendation engine, no behavioral tracking, no ads, and no infinite autoplay rabbit hole. Nothing reaches your child's feed unless you have admitted it. Strangers and algorithmic "reach" simply cannot get in.

Instead of letting a platform decide what your child watches, Kubo uses a web of trust: you build a viewing environment shaped by the family, friends, schools, creators, and communities you actually know. If the trust information can't be loaded, the feed fails closed and shows nothing — it never falls back to unvetted content.

Kubo is built on Nostr, an open and decentralized protocol, and is the reference implementation of TEPP, the Trust Extended Permissions Protocol. Because it is open, your family is never locked into one company's servers or business model.

What you get:
• A parent-curated video feed for your child — no recommendation engine
• A web of trust: add the people, schools, and creators you already know
• Trust on by design — the feed is protected from the very first launch and can't be switched off
• No behavioral ads, no engagement tracking, no dark patterns
• A calm, finite feed — no bottomless scroll, no autoplay traps
• Open source (AGPL-3.0) and built on open protocols

Kubo is a tool for parents. You set it up on a device you supervise and decide exactly who and what your child can see. It is designed to be operated by an adult, not configured by a child.

Kubo is published by the Web of Trust Foundation, a nonprofit building open tools that put families and communities — not algorithms — in control of what they see.
